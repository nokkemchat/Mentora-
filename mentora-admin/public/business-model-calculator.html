Suggested go-to-market and pricing structure

Use these as commercial starting points to validate with customers, not fixed market prices.

Schools
Monthly / annual subscription
  • Tier by active students or marking-credit allowance
  • Charge extra for usage beyond included credits
  • Offer a small pilot before annual commitment
  • Include teacher review, class reports and feedback
Universities
Annual institutional licence + usage
  • Department or faculty rollout
  • Usage bands based on scripts per year
  • SSO, LMS integration, audit trails and reporting
  • Paid onboarding and integration where needed
Examination boards
Paid pilot + multi-year enterprise contract
  • Platform / implementation fee plus per-script pricing
  • Volume tiers and committed annual minimums
  • Human moderation, validation and audit reporting
  • Security, support and service-level commitments
Pricing principle: avoid a once-off lifetime payment for an examination board. A one-time implementation fee can make sense, but ongoing inference, hosting, maintenance, support, security and quality assurance need recurring revenue. For schools, a subscription is usually easier to budget for; for boards, a contracted annual minimum plus usage-based pricing better matches variable exam volumes.

Assumptions and checks before using this with investors

  • The 2025 starting benchmark of 338,701 combines reported O-Level and A-Level candidates; it is not the verified total of scripts and excludes Grade 7. Candidate counts and script counts must not be treated as interchangeable.
  • Human cost per script defaults to $1.20 as a scenario value within reported media estimates of roughly $1.00–$1.80 for selected subjects. It is not an official blended average or audited annual spend.
  • AI cost inputs ($0.50 for edited marked images and $0.10 for annotations) are supplied estimates. Confirm whether they include all model, image processing, storage, retries, review and infrastructure costs.
  • Direct savings compare the assumed human marking cost with AI marking cost only. They do not automatically equal cash savings to an examination board: staffing, examiner allowances, quality review and other costs may not fall proportionally.
  • Throughput (16 / 32 / 60 papers per minute) is based on supplied estimates for 8-page papers. Real-world throughput and reliability need load testing.
  • Customer price, adoption share and fixed operating costs are editable scenario assumptions, not a market-validated pricing recommendation.
